Senior Defense 01 How do you guarantee the LLM will not hallucinate invalid diff line numbers?
Testing: Operational risks of LLM hallucinations & deterministic guardrails.
We enforce a zero-trust boundary around the model. First, temperature is set to 0.1 for deterministic decoding with a strict JSON schema. Second, in Stage 8, our Go Review Worker parses the git diff hunk headers into a hash set of added lines (+ lines). If the LLM proposes a comment on an unchanged or non-existent line, it is safely pruned before calling GitHub's API. This guarantees 100% delivery reliability and eliminates HTTP 422 errors.
Senior Defense 02 Your raw embeddings show 99% cosine similarity for unrelated code. Is your vector search broken?
Testing: Understanding of Transformer representation degeneration (BERT Anisotropy Problem).
That is the well-documented Representation Degeneration Problem in BERT models. Pre-trained embeddings occupy a narrow cone in high-dimensional vector space, skewing raw cosine scores above 0.85 even for different functions. In search.py, we implemented a calibrated piecewise linear transformation that remaps the active 0.70–0.99 anisotropic cluster into a realistic 50%–99% spread, restoring meaningful differentiation.

Senior Defense 01 Could your model hallucinate false anatomy or erase hairline fractures?
Testing: Clinical safety awareness and hallucination risk in generative vs residual models.
Generative models (like GANs or Diffusion models) suffer from hallucination risks because they sample from latent priors. DenoiseRX is strictly a deterministic residual regressor: y = x + f(x). It does not synthesize high-frequency textures from noise; it acts strictly as a spatially constrained non-linear noise subtraction filter. Furthermore, our SSIM score of 0.9251 proves high-contrast bone margins remain uncorrupted.
Senior Defense 02 How does your pipeline handle radiographs with odd dimensions (e.g. 1021×1021)?
Testing: The "Rule of 16" in 4-level U-Net downsampling hierarchies.
NAFNet utilizes a 4-level U-Net hierarchy where feature maps halve at each step ($2^4 = 16$). Odd dimensions cause integer floor division mismatches during skip-connection concatenation. In inference.py, we implement reflection padding (pad_to_multiple(x, 16)) to preserve border continuity without creating black boundary halos, then unpad before output delivery.

Senior Defense 01 Calling batch_df.collect() pulls data to the driver node. Isn't that an anti-pattern in Spark?
Testing: Distributed Big Data architecture fundamentals & honesty about bottlenecks.
Yes, in general distributed Big Data architecture, calling .collect() is an anti-pattern because it centralizes data onto the driver node, bypassing executor parallelism and risking Driver OOM. In this architecture, it represents a conscious prototyping trade-off: our 10-second micro-batches contain thousands of edges, easily fitting into memory. For 100x scale, the correct architectural evolution is to eliminate .collect() and execute distributed motif finding using Spark GraphFrames across worker executors.
Senior Defense 02 What happens if fraudsters space out their messages across 15 seconds instead of 10?
Testing: Understanding windowing boundaries and edge-splitting in stream processing.
This is the classic tumbling window boundary problem. If an attack is split across second 8 and second 12, non-overlapping 10-second micro-batches divide the edges, potentially dropping measured density below the 0.6 threshold. The production evolution is to replace tumbling micro-batches with a stateful sliding window (e.g. a 60-second window evaluated every 10 seconds) using Spark Structured Streaming watermarks.
